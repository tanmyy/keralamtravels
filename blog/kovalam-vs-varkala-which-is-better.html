Kovalam vs Varkala: Which Is Better?

Neither beach is objectively better; they serve different trips. Kovalam is the classic resort beach: calmer water, three crescent beaches, the lighthouse, big Ayurveda resorts, and 13 to 16 km from Trivandrum airport. Varkala is the cheaper, moodier alternative: a 30 to 40 meter laterite cliff dropping straight into the sea, backpacker hostels and cliff-top cafes, and a temple town older than its tourism. Want easy and comfortable? Pick Kovalam. Want atmosphere and a smaller bill? Pick Varkala.

The beaches themselves

Kovalam is a string of three crescent beaches: Lighthouse Beach at the southern end, Hawa Beach in the middle, and Samudra Beach to the north. The water here is relatively calm and shallow, which is why Kovalam has been a package-holiday favorite for decades. The red-and-white lighthouse on the headland is the landmark, and sunset walks along Lighthouse Beach are the town's signature ritual.

Varkala's beach is one long stretch of sand backed by the cliff: a 30 to 40 meter laterite wall that runs almost the entire length of town. You climb down stairs from the clifftop to reach the sand, and the view from the top is genuinely one of the best coastal views in Kerala. The sea here is stronger than at Kovalam. Lifeguards raise a red flag on rough days, so check conditions before going in.

Crowd and vibe

Kovalam is the polished, easy beach. Visitors skew older: family groups, wellness travelers, and foreign package tourists. The atmosphere is orderly, with sunbeds, beachside restaurants, and organized Ayurveda centers. Evenings wind down with dinners and soft music at the resorts. It is the beach you pick when you want everything handled.

Varkala runs younger and more independent. The North Cliff strip is a dense line of cafes, yoga shalas, handicraft shops, and homestays, and the social scene is backpacker-friendly: traveler meetups, occasional acoustic nights, and sunset gatherings on the cliff. Underneath the tourist strip there is a real pilgrim layer: the 2,000-year-old Janardanaswamy Temple and Papanasam Beach, a spot where pilgrims come to perform ancestral rites, sit at the northern end. That mix keeps Varkala from feeling like a resort bubble.

Stay costs compared

Varkala is clearly the cheaper town. 2026 travel-cost trackers put budget hostels and guesthouses at Rs 500 to Rs 1,000 per night, mid-range hotels at Rs 1,500 to Rs 3,000, and sea-view cliffside rooms at Rs 3,000 to Rs 6,000. A solo traveler can run a three-day Varkala trip for Rs 5,000 to Rs 10,000 all in.

Kovalam starts a rung higher and climbs further. Mid-range beachside hotels near Lighthouse Beach list around Rs 1,800 to Rs 2,700 per night in 2026 listings, and the big Ayurveda resorts and heritage properties price well above that. Budget rooms exist in Kovalam but they sit back from the beach, not on it.

Food scene

Varkala's cliff is its restaurant strip. Cafes serve everything from smoothie bowls and vegan thalis to Kerala meals with a sea view, and prices stay backpacker-friendly. Kovalam's dining is more structured: beachfront seafood restaurants, hotel restaurants, and tidy cafes. The quality is consistent and the setting is nicer, but expect to pay for it. Vegetarians are well served in both towns.

Getting there

Kovalam wins on access by a clear margin. Trivandrum International Airport is 13 to 16 km away, about 30 minutes by road, so you can land and be on the sand before sunset. Varkala is 43 to 51 km north of the airport, roughly 1 to 1.5 hours by cab, or a short train ride from Thiruvananthapuram followed by a short taxi. Best time to visit

Both beaches share the same sweet window: October to March, when the sea is calm and the humidity drops. Varkala runs a touch breezier thanks to the cliff. June to August is the monsoon: rough seas, many beachfront shacks closed, but the lowest room rates of the year. December to February is peak season with the highest prices at both beaches. So which is better for you?

  • Families and first-time Kerala visitors: Kovalam. Calmer water, resort-style security, and the shortest airport transfer.
  • Couples who want comfort: Kovalam, for the lighthouse sunsets and a beachfront resort. Couples who want character should look at Varkala's cliff-view rooms and candlelight dinners on the North Cliff.
  • Solo travelers and backpackers: Varkala, no contest. Cheaper, more social, and full of things to do on foot.
  • Wellness seekers: Kovalam for full Ayurveda resort programs with resident doctors; Varkala for casual, budget-friendly Ayurvedic massages by the cliff.
  • Short trips and tight schedules: Kovalam, because the 30-minute airport transfer saves you half a day on both ends.

If you have the days, do both. The two towns are roughly 60 to 70 km apart, about 1.5 to 2 hours by cab, and they complement each other: Kovalam as the resort beach for a night, Varkala as the cliff beach for two. For trip length planning, see how many days are enough for a Kerala first trip.

FAQs

Is Varkala or Kovalam better for swimming?

Kovalam. The water is calmer and shallower, with fewer rough days. Varkala's sea is stronger and the red flag goes up on rough days.

Which is cheaper, Kovalam or Varkala?

Varkala, by a wide margin. Budget stays start at Rs 500 to Rs 1,000 per night and daily food runs Rs 300 to Rs 700, against Kovalam's mid-range beachside rooms at Rs 1,800 to Rs 2,700.

Can I visit both Kovalam and Varkala in one trip?

Yes. They are about 60 to 70 km apart, roughly 1.5 to 2 hours by cab. One night at Kovalam and two at Varkala makes a good beach leg at the end of a Trivandrum-side trip.

Which is better for Ayurveda treatments?

Kovalam has the upscale Ayurveda resorts with resident doctors and full programs. Varkala has more casual, budget-friendly Ayurvedic massages along the cliff.

Is there any nightlife?

Neither town is a party scene. Kovalam offers quiet beachfront dinners and mellow resort bars. Varkala has a slightly livelier cliff scene: cafes with acoustic sets and occasional fire shows, soulful rather than loud.

Honest caveat: room rates move with the season and the year. Treat every figure here as a verified 2026 starting point, not a quote, and check current listings before booking.
