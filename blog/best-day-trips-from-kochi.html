Best Day Trips from Kochi

The five best day trips from Kochi are Athirappilly waterfalls, Cherai Beach, Kumbalangi village, Thattekad bird sanctuary, and Fort Kochi with Mattancherry inside the city itself. Athirappilly is the standout full-day trip: about 70 km away, open 8 AM to 6 PM, entry Rs 50 per Indian adult. Cherai is the cheapest at 25 km with free beach entry. Kumbalangi is the closest at 15 km with no village entry fee. Thattekad is for birders, and Fort Kochi needs no travel day at all.

Last updated: October 2026

1. Athirappilly and Vazhachal waterfalls

This is the day trip most Kochi visitors end up taking, and it earns the effort. Athirappilly is Kerala's largest waterfall, dropping about 80 feet, and Kerala Tourism puts it roughly 70 km from Kochi, about a 1.5 to 2 hour drive through Chalakudy. The ticket centre runs 8 AM to 6 PM, and one ticket covers Vazhachal Falls as well, less than 5 km up the same road, where the Chalakudy river rushes over a wide bed of rocks instead of dropping straight down.

Entry is not the expensive part. The Vazhachal Forest Division booking list, checked 8 October 2026, shows Rs 50 for an Indian adult, Rs 15 for a child aged 5 to 12, Rs 10 for a student, Rs 200 for a foreigner, Rs 60 for a camera and Rs 30 for a car. Book online up to 60 days ahead, carry your original government ID, and plan to be driving back toward Chalakudy by about 3 PM: the forest road does not allow night traffic. Our full breakdown with cab, bus and train options is in the Athirappilly day trip from Kochi cost post.

2. Cherai Beach, Vypeen island

Cherai is the easiest win on this list: about 25 km from the city centre, roughly 45 minutes to an hour away on Vypeen island, and entry is free. It is unusual because the sea and the backwaters exist side by side here, separated by coconut groves and paddy fields, and early mornings sometimes bring dolphin sightings off the shore. The beach has changing rooms, restrooms, parking and lifeguards, and the seafood shacks along the walkway make it a lunch stop as well as a swimming stop.

Go on a weekday morning if you can, and stay for the sunset, which is the beach's best hour. Getting there is simple: a cab or auto from the city, or local buses toward Vypeen followed by local transport to the beach. Guided half-day Cherai tours with hotel pickup are listed at about $50 for two people on travel booking sites. Best season is October to April, when the sea is calmer.

3. Kumbalangi village, India's first eco-tourism village

Kumbalangi is only 15 km from Kochi city, per Kerala Tourism, which makes it the closest day trip on this list. It is a working fishing village that Kerala picked as a model rural-tourism project back in 2003, and it is now branded as India's first eco-tourism village: Chinese fishing nets along the water, mangrove-lined canals, coir and basket-weaving demonstrations, the Kalagramam artists' village, and a famous after-the-rains phenomenon, the bioluminescent "sea sparkle" or kavaru in the backwaters around September and October.

There is no official village entry fee, so you can do this trip DIY: drive or take an auto the 30 to 40 minutes out, walk the village, eat at a homestay. If you want the organised version, guided full-day Kumbalangi tours on travel booking sites are listed from roughly $78 to $185 per person depending on length and inclusions, and one travel guide lists a hotel-led private tour from Taj Malabar at INR 12,000 plus taxes for up to 3 guests. Those are operator listings, not an official tariff, so confirm what is included before you pay. Many houses are homestays, so the trip easily stretches into an overnight if you want it to.

4. Thattekad bird sanctuary

For birders this is the serious day trip: Thattekad, also called the Dr. Salim Ali Bird Sanctuary, sits on the Periyar river near Kothamangalam, inland and east of Kochi. Established in 1983 and spread over about 25 square kilometres of evergreen and deciduous forest, it holds more than 300 recorded bird species, including the Malabar Grey Hornbill and the Indian Pitta. It opens 6 AM to 6 PM daily, so an early start is rewarded with the morning bird activity.

Entry is Rs 10 for Indian nationals and Rs 100 for foreign tourists, per the sanctuary's published fee list, and travel listings quote jeep safaris from about Rs 500 depending on duration and route. This is the trip to pick in winter, when migratory birds are around and the forest is at its most comfortable.

5. Fort Kochi and Mattancherry, the zero-travel day

You do not need to leave Kochi to have a day trip. Fort Kochi and Mattancherry pack Chinese fishing nets, St. Francis Church, the Santa Cruz Basilica, the Dutch Palace in Mattancherry and the spice warehouses of Jew Town into one walkable loop, and this is the best bad-weather fallback on the list. The exact entry fees by stop are covered in our Kochi 1 day itinerary post.

The honest version: this is a half-day at sightseeing pace and a full day if you add a Kathakali show or a cooking class. Since it costs nothing in transport, pair it with the afternoon run out to Kumbalangi, which is only 15 km away, for a full day that mixes city and village without a long drive.

How to get around on these day trips

Transport is the expensive part of every trip on this list, more than entry ever is. For Athirappilly, the numbers are firm: one-way sedan cabs are listed from about Rs 2,078 to Rs 2,100, so a day-hire with waiting and the return will cost more, while the KSRTC bus via Chalakudy starts from Rs 82 per person with a change for the last 30 km. For Cherai and Kumbalangi, app cabs and autos are the simplest since the distances are short. Whatever you choose, get the day's total in writing before you set off: pickup point, car type and whether the driver waits all change the final quote.

Frequently asked questions

Which day trip from Kochi is the cheapest?

Cherai Beach. Entry is free, it is only 25 km from the city, and you can go by bus and auto. Kumbalangi is the other cheap option: no village entry fee and only 15 km away.

Can I do Athirappilly and Cherai in one day?

No, not comfortably. They are in opposite directions, and the Athirappilly forest road needs you out by mid-afternoon. Do each on its own day.

Is one day enough for Fort Kochi and Mattancherry?

Yes. It is a half-day of sightseeing at a relaxed pace, and a full day if you add an evening Kathakali show. Our Kochi 1 day itinerary has the timings and entry fees.

Do I need to book anything in advance?

Book Athirappilly entry online on the Vazhachal Forest Division booking site, up to 60 days ahead, and carry your ID. Guided Kumbalangi tours need booking; Cherai, Thattekad and Fort Kochi need none.

What is the best day trip from Kochi for families with kids?

Cherai Beach for shallow water and changing rooms, or Kumbalangi for the fishing nets, boat rides and craft demos. Athirappilly involves a steep path down toward the base, which is hard for very young children.

When is the best time of year for these day trips?

October to March for all of them. Athirappilly is at its most dramatic just after the monsoon, Cherai's sea is calmest in these months, and Thattekad gets migratory birds in winter.

Honest caveat: entry fees above were checked against the Vazhachal Forest Division booking list (8 Oct 2026), Kerala Tourism's Kumbalangi page, and sanctuary and beach listings in October 2026. Cab figures are operator starting prices, not quotes. Forest staff can close Athirappilly viewpoints in heavy rain, so confirm fees, timings and closures on the day you travel.
