Thekkady 2 Day Itinerary: Budget, Entry Fees, and What to Do

A 2-day Thekkady trip costs roughly Rs 3,500 to 5,000 per person on a tight budget, Rs 6,000 to 9,000 per person for a comfortable mid-range trip (1 night, room shared by two). The big line item is the Periyar boating: Rs 255 per adult plus a Rs 45 reserve entry fee, so about Rs 330 per person all in. Book your boat slot online before you reach Kumily, because peak-season slots sell out.

Thekkady is a two-activity town: the Periyar Lake boat cruise and everything that grows in the hills around it. Two days is exactly enough for both, and it pairs naturally with Munnar (85 km away). Below is a day-by-day plan built around the actual fees the reserve charges, followed by stay tiers and a line-item budget.

Day 1: Arrive, spice plantation, Kathakali or Kalari in the evening

Arrive in Kumily by early afternoon. Thekkady town is small, so check in and head straight to a spice plantation for a guided walk. Entry to most plantations is free, and guided tours run about Rs 100 to 500 per person for a 1 to 2 hour walk through cardamom, pepper, cinnamon, and clove plantings. Guides explain the cultivation and you can taste and buy at the plantation shop; prices there run higher than the local market, so compare before you stock up.

In the evening, catch one of Thekkady's cultural shows. The Kathakali and Kalaripayattu centres in Kumily sell tickets separately for each show at Rs 200 per person, about an hour each. Book at the counter a day ahead if you want good seats, since popular shows sell out. Have dinner in Kumily town, where restaurants serve Kerala meals, appam and stew, and North Indian staples at town prices.

Day 2: Periyar boating, morning walk if time, then depart

Take the earliest boat slot you can get. Boats run at 7:30 AM, 9:30 AM, 11:15 AM, 1:45 PM, and 3:30 PM, and the cruise lasts about 90 minutes. Arrive at least 30 minutes early: private vehicles cannot go past the parking area, and you need to clear entry, the forest shuttle bus, and the boarding queue. Morning and late-afternoon slots give the best wildlife odds; expect elephants, sambar, and wild boar on the banks, and go for the scenery, because tiger sightings from the boat are rare.

If you stayed a second night, the reserve also runs guided nature walks (3 hours, Rs 350 per person) and bamboo rafting (Rs 1,800 per person), both bookable through the Periyar Foundation eco-tourism desk. Otherwise, head out after lunch. Distances: Munnar is about 2.5 hours, Alleppey about 3 hours, and Kochi about 5 hours by road.

Add your transport to Thekkady on top: a KSRTC bus from Kochi costs a few hundred rupees, while a private taxi for the full 2 days runs into the thousands. The boat is the one cost nobody should skip; everything else can flex.

Money-saving tips

  • Book the Forest Department boat, not the KTDC one, if price matters; the cruise is the same lake.
  • Take the earliest or latest slot for the best wildlife viewing, which is free value.
  • Side seats on the boat give better views and photos than middle rows; board early.
  • Buy your Kathakali and Kalari tickets directly at the centre, not through hotel middlemen.
  • Compare spice prices at the Kumily market before buying at a plantation shop.

FAQs

Is 2 days enough for Thekkady?

Yes, for the highlights. The boat cruise plus a spice plantation and one evening show fit neatly into two days. Add a third day only if you want a bamboo rafting session or a tiger trail trek, both of which need half to a full day.

Can I book Periyar boating online?

Yes. Both the Forest Department and KTDC offer online pre-booking, and slots, especially the 7:30 AM and 3:30 PM ones, sell out in peak season (October to February). Carry a valid ID and keep the ticket handy on your phone.

How much does the Thekkady boat ride cost?

Rs 255 per adult and Rs 85 for children under 11 on Forest Department boats, plus the Rs 45 reserve entry and about Rs 30 for the forest shuttle bus. KTDC boats cost roughly Rs 450 per person.

What is the best time to visit Thekkady?

October to February is the sweet spot: cool, clear weather and good wildlife sightings. The monsoon (June to September) brings lush greenery but rain can interrupt boat schedules. For the full month-by-month picture, see our best time to visit Kerala month by month guide.

Is Thekkady good for kids?

Yes. The boat cruise is calm, the plantation walks are easy, and children under 5 ride the boat free. The Kalari show is usually the bigger hit with kids than Kathakali.

Should I combine Thekkady with Munnar?

If you have 4 days, yes: Munnar first for the tea hills, then Thekkady for wildlife and spices. Our Munnar 2-day itinerary budget guide uses the same line-item format, so you can price the combined trip.
