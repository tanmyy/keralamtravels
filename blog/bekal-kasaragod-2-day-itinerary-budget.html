Bekal and Kasaragod 2 Day Itinerary

Two days in Bekal and Kasaragod for 2 people costs about Rs 5,200 to Rs 10,700 self-planned for 1 night, excluding travel to Kasaragod. Sightseeing is cheap: DTPC Kasaragod lists Bekal Fort entry at Rs 20 per person, open 8 am to 6 pm, and Bekal Beach Park next door charges Rs 30 per person. Spend day 1 on Bekal Fort, the beach park and Kappil Beach, about 6 km away, and day 2 on Chandragiri Fort, the Kasaragod town sights and an optional Valiyaparamba backwater outing, about 25 to 30 km south.

Last updated: October 2026

Bekal is a small coastal town in Kasaragod, Kerala's northernmost district, about 16 km south of Kasaragod town. Its fort is the largest in Kerala, covering about 40 acres on a headland above the Arabian Sea, with almost three-quarters of its outer wall water-bound, according to DTPC Kasaragod. The district spreads its sights out, so a good 2 day plan groups the coast on day 1 and keeps day 2 flexible, rather than criss-crossing the district.

Two honest notes. First, some travel sites list the fort ticket at Rs 25 for adults with free entry under 15, while DTPC Kasaragod, the district tourism body, lists Rs 20 per person. Carry small cash and expect one of those two figures at the gate. Second, the luxury resorts change the maths completely: one night at the Taj Bekal example costs more than the entire mid-range trip, so pick your stay tier first and build the rest around it.

How to reach Bekal and Kasaragod

  1. By train: Kasaragod and Kanhangad are the two main stations, each about 20 to 30 minutes by road from the fort. The small Bekal Fort and Kotikulam stations are closer, but only a few passenger and express trains stop there, so most visitors use Kasaragod or Kanhangad and finish by auto or bus.
  2. By air: Mangalore International Airport is the nearest, roughly 65 km away and about 1.5 hours by road. Kannur International Airport is the in-state alternative, roughly 70 to 100 km depending on the route. There is no airport in Kasaragod district.
  3. By road: Bekal sits just off NH66, the Kochi to Mumbai coastal highway. Buses connect Kasaragod town with Mangalore, Kannur and Kozhikode, and frequent local buses and autos cover the roughly 16 km from Kasaragod town to Bekal.

Stay near the fort in Pallikere if the fort and beach are your priority: a Kasaragod town hotel adds 20 to 30 minutes of driving to the fort each way, but puts the station, buses and cheaper food on your doorstep.

Day 1: Bekal Fort, Bekal Beach Park and Kappil Beach

Morning, Bekal Fort. Arrive near the 8 am opening, while it is cool and quiet. DTPC Kasaragod lists the fort as open all days from 8 am to 6 pm at Rs 20 per person, with offline tickets only. Allow 2 to 3 hours: the keyhole-shaped laterite fort, built on a steep hill about 130 ft above sea level, has a zigzag entrance, a water tank and an observation tower with the best sea view in the district. There is little shade on the ramparts, so carry water and a cap.

Midday, Bekal Beach and the beach park. The beach and its park sit right beside the fort, a short walk away. The park lists entry at Rs 30 per person, hours 8 am to 11 pm, with rides, a kids' play area and a food court, which makes it the easy family stop. Take lunch here or at a local restaurant nearby and try the coastal food: fish curry meals, mussel fry and Malabar biryani are the Kasaragod staples.

Evening, Kappil Beach. About 6 km north of the fort, Kappil is quieter and less developed than Bekal Beach, with golden sand, casuarina trees and the Kodi Cliff viewpoint above it. Go for sunset, when the light turns over the sea and the backwater behind the beach. If you skipped the park, Pallikere Beach, just south of the fort, is the quieter alternative for an evening walk.

Day 2: Chandragiri Fort, Kasaragod town and the backwaters option

Morning, Chandragiri Fort and Kasaragod town. Drive north to Chandragiri Fort, the smaller 17th-century fort on the river near Kasaragod town, then cover the town sights that interest you: the Malik Dinar Mosque, one of the oldest in the region, and the Ananthapura Lake Temple further inland if you have a car and an early start. Keep this morning loose: the district's sights are spread out, and rushing them is the common mistake.

Afternoon, choose one: Valiyaparamba backwaters or Ranipuram. Valiyaparamba, near Nileshwaram about 25 to 30 km south, is Kasaragod's backwater stretch, with day houseboat cruises: confirm the boarding point, cruise length, meals and current price before you pay, as operators and rates vary and none are published on an official tariff page. Ranipuram, the inland hill station, is the other option, but it is a separate outing with a hill drive and a walk on uneven ground, so only pick it if you start early and skip the backwaters. Trying to do both in one afternoon means spending it in the car.

Evening, depart or stay a second night. Evening trains from Kasaragod and Kanhangad connect south toward Kannur, Kozhikode and Kochi, and north toward Mangalore. If your train is late, a second night near the fort costs from about Rs 2,300 in town to Rs 4,600 near the beach at the listing anchors above. Prices move with season and weekends, and the October to March window is the busy one. Book the near-fort stays early for holiday weekends; town hotels are the fallback that rarely sells out.

Best time to visit Bekal and Kasaragod

October to March is the best window: cooler weather for walking the fort ramparts and the beaches, and calmer seas. June to September is the monsoon, when heavy rain makes fort walks slippery and beach plans unreliable. Summer, April and May, is hot and humid on an exposed laterite fort, so sightsee early and late. For the statewide picture, see best time to visit Kerala, month by month. Pack sunscreen, a cap and light cotton for the fort, and modest dress if you plan temple or mosque visits in town.

FAQ

How much does a 2 day Bekal and Kasaragod trip cost for 2 people?

About Rs 5,200 to Rs 6,200 on the budget version (town hotel, local food) and about Rs 9,100 to Rs 10,700 mid-range (stay near the fort), for 1 night and 2 days, checked October 2026. Travel to Kasaragod and any backwater cruise are extra, and a luxury resort night starts around Rs 23,620 on current listings.

What is the Bekal Fort entry fee and timing?

DTPC Kasaragod lists Rs 20 per person, open all days from 8 am to 6 pm, with offline tickets only. Some travel sites list Rs 25 for adults and free entry under 15, so carry small cash and confirm at the gate.

How do I reach Bekal Fort by train?

Use Kasaragod or Kanhangad, the main stations about 20 to 30 minutes by road from the fort, then an auto or bus. Bekal Fort and Kotikulam stations are closer, but only a few trains stop there.

Is 2 days enough for Bekal and Kasaragod?

Yes, for the fort, the beach park, Kappil Beach and one of the backwater or hill outings. Add a third day if you want both Valiyaparamba and Ranipuram without rushing, as each is a separate outing in a different direction.

Are Bekal and Kasaragod the same place?

No. Bekal is the coastal town and fort about 16 km south of Kasaragod town, inside Kasaragod district. Check which one your hotel is actually in before you book, because it changes your drive time to the fort and the station.

Can I swim at Bekal Beach?

The beach park area is supervised and popular for paddling, but open-sea swimming on this coast is limited by currents. Follow the local guards' advice on the day, stay in the supervised stretch, and treat the beach mainly as a walk and sunset stop.
