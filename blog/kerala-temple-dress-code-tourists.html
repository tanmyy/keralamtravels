Kerala Temple Dress Code for Tourists

The short answer: Kerala's major temples enforce a strict traditional dress code. Men wear a white mundu (dhoti) with no shirt or upper garment; women wear a saree, set-mundu, or salwar kameez. Jeans, shorts, and t-shirts will get you turned away at the gate. The strictest is Sree Padmanabhaswamy Temple in Thiruvananthapuram, where a Kerala High Court ruling keeps entry for women saree-only. Guruvayur also allows churidar. Mundus can be rented or bought from stalls near the entrances, so a dress-code surprise does not have to cancel your visit.

The quick answer, temple by temple

Rules differ from temple to temple, and the two big ones tourists visit most are also the strictest. Here is how they compare.

Sree Padmanabhaswamy Temple: the strictest dress code in Kerala

The Padmanabhaswamy Temple in Thiruvananthapuram, famous as one of India's richest temples, has the strictest dress rules in the state. Men must wear a dhoti-style white mundu and remove any shirt or upper garment before entering; an angavastram (a cloth draped over the shoulders) is permitted. Trousers, shorts, and t-shirts are not allowed.

For women, the rule is saree only. In December 2016, the Kerala High Court barred churidar and salwar kameez inside the temple, striking down a brief relaxation that had briefly allowed churidar. Women who arrive in other outfits can wrap a mundu over their dress, and visitors report being able to borrow or rent one at the counter beside the temple.

Entry is restricted to Hindus, footwear must be left outside, and photography is strictly prohibited inside the temple, including the inner sanctum and corridors. Mobile phones and bags are also banned; use the cloakroom counters outside. Reported darshan windows are 3:30 to 4:45 AM, 6:30 to 7:00 AM, 8:30 to 10:00 AM, and 10:30 to 11:15 AM in the morning, with evening darshan from 4:30 to 6:10 PM and 6:45 to 7:20 PM, but timings shift during festivals, so confirm before you go. Arriving for the early morning slots means far smaller crowds.

Guruvayur Temple: the rules most Kerala temples follow

Guruvayur, the Krishna temple in Thrissur district, sets the template that many Kerala temples follow. Men enter in a clean white mundu and stay bare-chested; shirts, vests, trousers, and printed lungis are prohibited. An angavastram drape over the upper body is acceptable.

Women must wear traditional attire: a saree, the Kerala set-mundu, or a salwar kameez with dupatta. Jeans, leggings, shorts, and modern western clothing are not permitted. Unlike Padmanabhaswamy, Guruvayur does allow churidar for women, which makes it the easier temple for travellers who packed a salwar suit.

Children under 12 get some relaxation: boys may wear shorts or a mundu, and girls may wear a pavada, churidar, or long frock. Entry inside the sanctum is restricted to Hindus; non-Hindus can visit the surroundings but not enter the temple. Mobile phones, smartwatches, and cameras are completely banned inside the premises. Approximate temple hours are 3:00 AM to 12:30 PM and 4:30 PM to 9:15 PM, with the biggest crowds at the early morning and evening darshans.

What about other Kerala temples?

Most Kerala temples follow the same base pattern: a mundu without a shirt for men, traditional attire for women, footwear left at the entrance. Smaller village temples may be less strict about inspecting every visitor, but the expectation still holds, and following it is read as basic respect. The one safe rule for your whole trip: assume every temple you enter requires the mundu-and-no-shirt dress for men and traditional dress for women, then treat Guruvayur and Padmanabhaswamy as the two that enforce it gate-by-gate.

Where to rent or buy a mundu near the temple

Forgot to pack one? Stalls and shops near the entrances of the major temples rent out or sell mundus, and changing facilities are available nearby. These are visitor-reported figures, not official prices, so treat them as ballparks.

What is not allowed inside

Beyond clothing, both temples ban mobile phones, cameras, smartwatches, and bags inside. Footwear comes off before the premises. At Padmanabhaswamy, keys and helmets are also on the banned list, with cloakroom counters outside to hold them. Do not plan on taking photos inside either temple; photography is strictly prohibited, and the only pictures you can take home are the official ones sold at authorized shops outside.

Tips for foreign tourists

The dress code applies to everyone, including foreign visitors; there is no separate tourist exemption. Men should know that going bare-chested inside is the norm, not an indignity, and an angavastram drape is fine if that feels more comfortable. Women travelling light should note that a salwar kameez with dupatta passes at Guruvayur but not at Padmanabhaswamy, where only a saree or a wrapped mundu will do. Plan temple visits early in the morning, when the crowds are thinnest and the heat is kindest. Festival days like those in the peak season calendar draw enormous crowds, so check dates before you lock an itinerary.

FAQ

Can women wear a churidar to Padmanabhaswamy Temple? No. The Kerala High Court's December 2016 ruling requires women to wear a saree inside; churidar and salwar kameez are not permitted. Women in other outfits can wrap a mundu over their dress.

Can men wear shirts inside Kerala temples? No. Men must remove shirts, vests, and any stitched upper garment before entering the major temples. A white mundu is required, and an angavastram drape is optional.

Can non-Hindus enter Kerala temples? At Padmanabhaswamy and Guruvayur, entry inside the temple is restricted to Hindus. Other temples vary, so check the specific temple before planning.

Can I take photos inside the temple? No. Photography, mobile phones, and cameras are strictly prohibited inside Padmanabhaswamy and Guruvayur.

What do children wear to Kerala temples? Children under 12 get relaxed rules: boys may wear shorts or a mundu, and girls may wear a pavada, churidar, or a long frock.

Where do I leave my phone and bag? Cloakroom or deposit counters outside the temple entrance. Visitor reports put the charge at roughly Rs.5 to 30.
