How to Book an Alleppey Houseboat: the 2026 Checklist

Book directly with the operator through their website, phone, or WhatsApp, and get everything in writing before you pay any advance. For a private 1-bedroom overnight boat in 2026, expect Rs 10,000 to Rs 13,000 in the October-to-March season and Rs 7,000 to Rs 9,000 from April to September, with 5% GST usually added on top. Confirm the exact boat name, AC hours, every meal, boarding point, check-in and check-out times, and the cancellation policy in writing. Book at least two weeks ahead for peak-season dates, and three weeks ahead for mid-December to mid-January.

Last updated: October 2026

Step 1: Pick the right category and cruise type

Category names matter less than what they hide. A deluxe boat usually means air conditioning only at night, roughly 9 PM to 6 AM, while premium and luxury boats run AC full-time. If you are travelling between April and September, full-time AC is not a luxury, it is the difference between sleeping and sweating. The overnight version includes dinner and breakfast and the early-morning backwater light that day cruises miss. Step 2: Choose where you book

There are three honest routes, each with a tradeoff.

Book directly with the operator. This is the route most guides recommend: contact the company through its own website, phone, or WhatsApp so you can ask about the specific boat you will stay on and request recent photos or video, not just brochure shots. Direct booking also makes customisation easier, like a vegetarian kitchen or a specific route.

Book through an established online booking platform. Platforms that list named, verified boats give you a written terms sheet and a documented payment trail, which helps if something goes wrong.

Walk in at the jetty. Booking at the Finishing Point jetty lets you inspect the boat before committing: walk the deck, check the bathroom, look at the bedroom, and confirm the AC works. In the off-season (April to September), operators often accept 20 to 30 percent below their listed rate for same-day bookings. The risks: in peak season the good boats are gone, and touts at the jetty sometimes show luxury photos and put you on a budget boat.

Honest caveat: avoid random agents at the jetty who will not name the boat until you pay. Legitimate operators expect detailed questions and answer them willingly.

Step 3: Get these 10 things in writing before paying

This is the part people skip, and it is the part that prevents nearly every bad houseboat story. Get the answers on WhatsApp or email, not just on a phone call, before you transfer any advance.

  1. The exact boat name. A category like "deluxe" is not a booking. Names let you look up real guest reviews of that specific vessel.
  2. Private or shared. On a shared boat you book one room and share the dining area and deck with strangers. For couples, private is worth it.
  3. Bedrooms, beds, and bathrooms. Bedrooms, bed configuration, and whether every bathroom is attached. Do not judge by total passenger capacity alone.
  4. AC hours and coverage. Night-only or full-time? Bedrooms only, or the living area too? Ask both questions, not just "is there AC?"
  5. Every meal included. The standard overnight package is welcome drink, lunch, evening tea with snacks, dinner, and breakfast. Confirm each one, plus dietary requests (most cooks handle vegetarian and can manage north Indian or Chinese food with advance notice), and whether seafood specials like karimeen or prawns cost extra.
  6. The route and cruising hours. Boats typically cruise 5 to 6 hours a day and stop cruising by about 5:30 PM under local operating rules, anchoring for the night near a village bank.
  7. Boarding point and timings. Overnight check-in is typically around noon with checkout at 9 AM the next day. Confirm the exact jetty, because Alleppey has more than one.
  8. The total price, itemised. Is 5% GST included or added on top? (Most operators charge it separately.) What do extra adults or children cost? One established operator's published policy treats children under 5 as complimentary and charges for children aged 5 to 11 needing an extra bed or meals.
  9. The advance and how to pay. Most operators take a portion as an advance to confirm and the balance on arrival. Pay through the operator's official payment link or published bank details, and keep the receipt.
  10. Cancellation and rescheduling terms. Ask what happens if the cruise is affected by severe weather, government restrictions, boat maintenance, or route limitations, and get the refund and rescheduling rules in writing.

Step 4: Pay the advance the right way

The usual structure is an advance payment to confirm your date and boat, with the balance settled before check-in. Three rules keep this safe: pay only to the operator's official account or payment link (never to a personal account a jetty tout gives you), get a written confirmation that names the boat, the check-in point, the timings, and the meal plan, and keep screenshots of every payment and the confirmation message.

Step 5: Inspect the boat on boarding day

Even with a written confirmation, spend ten minutes checking the boat before it casts off. Look at the bathroom and kitchen (these are where poorly maintained boats fail), confirm the AC runs and cools the bedroom, and check that safety equipment is on board: life jackets and fire extinguishers. Kerala Tourism Department licensed boats carry a visible license plate; the department classifies boats as platinum, gold, or silver based on safety standards, eco-compliance, and amenities, so the license is worth a glance. If what you see does not match what was promised in writing, you have documentation to push back with.

Red flags that mean walk away

A boat or operator is not worth your money if any of these apply: they will not tell you the exact boat name before payment; they only share exterior photos and refuse recent photos or video of the bedrooms, bathrooms, and kitchen; the quote is dramatically cheaper than every other operator for the same category (very cheap deals often compromise safety or maintenance); there are no written cancellation or refund terms; or an agent pressures you to pay cash at the jetty without letting you inspect the boat first.

How far in advance should you book?

Two weeks ahead is the minimum for the October-to-March season, and three weeks ahead for the December-to-January peak when rates spike. During holidays like Christmas, New Year, Onam, and long weekends, booking earlier also gives you more choice of category and bedroom count. For off-season dates, a few days ahead, or even a walk-in at the jetty, is usually fine.

Frequently asked questions

Is it cheaper to book at the jetty on arrival?

In the off-season, often yes: same-day walk-ins can negotiate 20 to 30 percent off listed rates. In peak season you trade choice for price, and the best boats book out weeks ahead. If you walk in, inspect the exact boat before paying.

Private or shared houseboat: which should I book?

Shared boats cost less per room, but you share the dining area, deck, and lobby with strangers. For couples, honeymooners, and families who want control over the experience, a private boat is the safer choice. See our day cruise vs overnight guide for how the two formats compare on price and experience.

Does the AC run all day?

Only on premium and luxury boats. On deluxe boats AC typically runs from about 9 PM to 6 AM, which is fine in the October-to-March season but uncomfortable in summer afternoons. Confirm the hours and which rooms are covered before paying.

What happens if it rains or the cruise is cancelled?

Monsoon rain rarely cancels cruises outright, but severe weather, government restrictions, or maintenance can change plans. This is exactly why step 3 asks for written cancellation and rescheduling terms: know whether you get a refund, a date change, or a substitute boat before money changes hands.

Are houseboats safe for families with kids?

Licensed boats carry life jackets and fire extinguishers, and decks have railings, but toddlers need constant supervision around water. Check bed configuration carefully so everyone has real sleeping space, and ask the operator about child safety arrangements for your group size.
