Wayanad 2 Day Itinerary Budget

Two days in Wayanad is enough for Edakkal Caves, Soochipara Falls, Banasura Sagar Dam and Pookode Lake if you base yourself in Kalpetta and group sights by direction. Expect roughly Rs 2,300 to 3,200 per person on a tight budget, or Rs 4,500 to 6,500 per person mid-range, for 2 days and 1 night with a room shared by two, excluding transport to reach Wayanad.

Last updated: October 2026

How to split the 2 days

Wayanad is a spread-out district, not a walkable town. Its sights sit in different directions from Kalpetta, so the trick is to pair them by road, not by fame. Day 1 covers the south side: Edakkal Caves near Ambalavayal, about 25 km from Kalpetta, paired with the Heritage Museum area or an early evening back in town. Day 2 covers the west and north-west: Soochipara Falls near Meppadi in the morning, then Banasura Sagar Dam and Pookode Lake on the way back. That order keeps backtracking low and leaves the steepest climbs for when you are freshest.

One warning shapes the whole plan: Edakkal Caves is closed on Mondays. If your two days include a Monday, swap the days around and do the falls and dam first.

Day 1: Edakkal Caves and Ambalavayal

Start early. Edakkal is open roughly 8:30 AM to 4:00 PM, entry is Rs 50 for Indian adults, Rs 30 for children up to 12, and Rs 100 for foreign nationals, with still camera charges extra and a jeep shuttle from the base at about Rs 70 per person. These are not true caves but a rock cleft on Ambukuthi hill, reached by a steep climb of steps and ladders that takes most visitors 2 to 3 hours in total. The reward is a set of petroglyphs several thousand years old, plus wide valley views from the top. Wear shoes with grip, carry water, and skip this climb if anyone in the group has serious knee or mobility trouble: the final stretch is genuinely steep.

After lunch in Ambalavayal or Sulthan Bathery, keep the afternoon light. The climb is the day: most people who try to stack a waterfall trek on top of it end up enjoying neither. Use the evening to walk Kalpetta town, buy coffee and spices, and eat early.

Day 2: Soochipara Falls, Banasura Sagar and Pookode Lake

Head to Soochipara Falls first, while the trail is cool. Current listings put entry at Rs 150 per person, with visiting hours roughly 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM, a three-tiered fall near Meppadi reached by a descending forest trail of about 2 km each way. Allow about 2 hours. The walk down is easy; the climb back up is the work. The pool at the base may be restricted when flow is high, and the falls run weak or may close in the dry months from March to June, so October to February is the reliable window.

From there, continue to Banasura Sagar Dam, one of the largest earthen dams in India. Entry is Rs 40 for adults and Rs 20 for children. The headline activity is the speed boat, at about Rs 1,000 for a boat of five, so roughly Rs 200 per person in a full group. Finish at Pookode Lake, about 13 km from Kalpetta, where entry is around Rs 40 per person and a two-seat pedal boat is around Rs 100. It is a gentle, family-friendly last stop before the drive out.

Where to stay: Wayanad stay tiers

Stay in or near Kalpetta. It sits between the Edakkal side and the Banasura-Pookode side, has the widest choice of food, and avoids a long night drive on ghat roads. Vythiri suits resort stays; Sulthan Bathery suits travellers continuing toward Mysore or Bangalore.

Getting to Wayanad is extra. Most travellers arrive via Kozhikode (Calicut), then take a bus or cab up the Thamarassery ghat road to Kalpetta. Tips that save time and money

1. Do Edakkal first thing in the morning: the climb is exposed and the queue builds by midday.

2. Never plan Edakkal on a Monday. It is the classic Wayanad itinerary mistake.

3. Hire a cab for Day 2 only. Day 1 is a single return trip, but Day 2 chains three sights and a cab pays for itself in saved hours.

4. Build your wider trip with the 4-day Wayanad trek itinerary if you add days for Chembra or the wildlife sanctuaries, and price the full Kerala route in the trip budget planner.

FAQ

Is 2 days enough for Wayanad?

For the headline sights, yes: Edakkal, Soochipara, Banasura and Pookode fit comfortably in 2 days from Kalpetta. What 2 days does not fit is the wildlife side: Muthanga or Tholpetty safaris and a Chembra Peak trek each need their own half or full day, so add a third day if those matter to you.

What is the best base: Kalpetta, Vythiri or Sulthan Bathery?

Kalpetta for a first 2-day trip, because it is central for both day plans and has the most food and transport options. Vythiri if the stay itself is the experience. Sulthan Bathery if you are driving on toward Mysore or Bangalore.

When is the best time for this itinerary?

October to February: Soochipara has water, trails are dry underfoot, and mornings are cool for the Edakkal climb. In peak monsoon, expect trail closures and restricted pool access at the falls.

Can I do this itinerary without a car?

Yes, with buses between towns and autos for the last stretches, but Day 2 becomes tight. The budget column above assumes buses and autos; if you value the Pookode sunset, take the cab that day.

Honest caveat: entry fees above were checked against current attraction listings in October 2026, but Wayanad counters do revise rates without much notice. Treat the totals as a planning budget, not a quote, and confirm at the ticket counter.
